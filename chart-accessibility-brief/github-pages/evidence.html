Evidence · Charts Without Guesswork

The research behind “describe the chart from the data.”

Each study opens with what it found, then the facts, the limits and the link. “Read in full” means I checked the numbers against the original paper. Current as of November 9, 2026.

00 / How to read the numbers

A benchmark score is per question. A description is many claims.

At 93% per claim, a description with five independent claims is fully right about 70% of the time.

0.93 × 0.93 × 0.93 × 0.93 × 0.93 ≈ 0.70

No published benchmark measures how often a full AI chart description is entirely correct. The closest evidence is the caption audit and the numeric check below.

01 / AI with the data supplied

Handed the data table, top models still misstated about 1 in 5 numbers.

Giving the model the data helped, but it did not make the numbers reliable.

Study
Chart-Supported or Model-Supplied? Examining MLLM-Generated Claims for Accessible Visualization (arXiv, September 2026)
Models
GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.5 Flash, Llama 4 Scout 17B
Scale
102 charts from 4 sources, 4 input conditions, 1,224 descriptions, 4,210 numbers checked
Result
Numbers matching the data within 2%: GPT-5.4 79.0%, Gemini 79.7%, Llama 71.3%, overall 77.5%
Also
Adding the image on top of the data gave no consistent gain. 73.9% of “real-world significance” claims relied on information outside the chart.

Limits. The check matches numbers only, so a matching number can still sit in a wrong sentence. Some mismatches may come from filtering or rounding. The models labeled their own claims.

02 / AI-written captions

82% of AI chart captions had at least one factual error.

People checked every sentence. Most captions had something wrong in them.

Study
Do LVLMs Understand Charts? Analyzing and Correcting Factual Errors in Chart Captioning (Huang et al., ACL Findings 2024)
Scale
1,187 captions and 5,323 sentences, annotated by people
Result
82.06% of captions had an error. GPT-4V and Bard: 81.27% of captions (321 of 395), about 43% of sentences. GPT-4 writing from a table extracted from the chart: 86% of captions.
Errors
Wrong values, wrong labels, wrong trend direction, exaggerated change, facts not in the chart

Limits. 2023-era models. Today's models are better, but no one has repeated this full-caption audit on 2026 models.

arxiv.org/abs/2312.10160Read in full

04 / Busy charts

Add more to the picture and the AI falls apart. People barely slow down.

With nine histograms overlaid, the model was right about 6% of the time. People stayed around 86%.

Test
Synthetic charts with no text, so the answer had to come from the shapes. Question: which histograms are left-skewed?
Layouts
Each histogram in its own panel, or all histograms overlaid in one chart, from 4 to 9 histograms
Separate
Claude 3.7 Sonnet about 88% falling to about 79%. People about 100% falling to about 93%.
Overlaid
Claude 3.7 Sonnet about 43% falling to about 6%. People about 100% falling to about 86%.

This matters for accessibility because busy charts are where screen-reader users depend on the description most. A rule-based description reads the data, so nine series cost it the same as one.

Limits. One model, 100 examples per question type, people drawn from the authors. Values read from the paper's Figure 2, so they are approximate.

05 / Leaderboard scores

On easier tests, top models now beat people. That is why those scores mislead.

  • CharXiv reasoning: top 2026 models about 93% on public leaderboards. People 80.5%. GPT-4o scored 47.1% at launch in 2024.
  • ChartQA and ChartQAPro: Claude 3.5 Sonnet scored 90.5% on ChartQA but 55.8% on the harder ChartQAPro. Self-reported 2026 scores on ChartQAPro are around 71%.

Limits. Leaderboards come from aggregators and vendors with different settings, and they change monthly. Quote them as approximate.

06 / Errors that build up

Over 20 delegated tasks, frontier models silently lost a quarter of the content.

The errors were rare per step, but severe, quiet and cumulative.

Study
LLMs Corrupt Your Documents When You Delegate (Laban, Schnabel and Neville, Microsoft Research, April 2026)
Scale
19 models, 52 professional domains, 310 work environments, 20 delegated edits each
Result
Content kept after 20 edits: Gemini 3.1 Pro 80.9%, Claude 4.6 Opus 73.1%, GPT 5.4 71.5%. All 19 models averaged about 50% degradation.
Ready
“Ready” means keeping 98% or more. The best model was ready in 11 of 52 domains.

Limits. About document editing, not charts. It shows why output that runs repeatedly without checks needs a safeguard.

arxiv.org/abs/2604.15597 · Microsoft Research blogRead in full

08 / What blind readers want

Blind readers asked for straight facts about the data.

Those facts are the easiest content to compute from the data, and the hardest to get right from a picture.

  • 30 blind and 90 sighted readers ranked chart descriptions. Most blind readers wanted descriptions to “stick to straight facts about the data.”
  • The authors' first two levels of content, what the chart encodes plus statistics such as extremes and comparisons, can be computed directly when software has the data. From an image it is “considerably more difficult.”
  • They recommend descriptions that convey overall trends and statistics.
