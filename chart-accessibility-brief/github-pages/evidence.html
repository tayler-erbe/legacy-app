How to read these numbers

A benchmark score is per question. A description makes many claims, so its chance of being fully right is lower.

If a model is right 93% of the time per claim and a description makes five independent claims, the chance that all five are right is about 70%.

0.93 × 0.93 × 0.93 × 0.93 × 0.93 ≈ 0.70

No published benchmark measures how often a full AI chart description is entirely correct. The closest evidence is the caption audit and the numeric check below.

AI with the data supplied

Even with the data table in hand, top 2026 models misstated about 1 in 5 numbers they called directly supported.

Study
Chart-Supported or Model-Supplied? Examining MLLM-Generated Claims for Accessible Visualization (arXiv, September 2026)
Models
GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.5 Flash, Llama 4 Scout 17B
Scale
102 charts from 4 sources, 4 input conditions, 1,224 descriptions, 4,210 numbers checked
Result
Numbers matching the data within 2%: GPT-5.4 79.0%, Gemini 79.7%, Llama 71.3%, overall 77.5%
Also
Adding the image to the data gave no consistent gain. 73.9% of “real-world significance” claims relied on information outside the chart.

Limits. The check matches numbers only, so a matching number can still sit in a wrong sentence. Some mismatches may come from filtering or rounding between the table and the chart. The models labeled their own claims.

AI-written chart captions

82% of AI chart captions had at least one factual error when people checked them.

Study
Do LVLMs Understand Charts? Analyzing and Correcting Factual Errors in Chart Captioning (Huang et al., ACL Findings 2024)
Scale
1,187 captions and 5,323 sentences, annotated by people
Result
82.06% of captions had an error. GPT-4V and Bard: 81.27% of captions (321 of 395), about 43% of sentences. A pipeline that turned the chart into a table, then had GPT-4 write: 86% of captions.
Error types
Wrong values, wrong labels, wrong trend direction, exaggerated change, facts not in the chart

Limits. These are 2023-era models. Today's models are better, but no one has repeated this full-caption audit on 2026 models.

Leaderboard scores

On easier, saturated tests, top 2026 models now beat people, which is why those scores mislead.

  • CharXiv (reasoning): top 2026 models about 93% on public leaderboards; people 80.5%; GPT-4o was 47.1% at launch in 2024.
  • ChartQA vs ChartQAPro: Claude 3.5 Sonnet scored 90.5% on ChartQA but 55.8% on the harder ChartQAPro. Self-reported 2026 scores on ChartQAPro are around 71%.

Limits. Leaderboard numbers come from aggregators and vendors with different settings and change monthly. Quote them as approximate.

Errors that build up

When AI runs again and again without checks, small errors add up. Frontier models lost 25% of content over 20 tasks.

Study
LLMs Corrupt Your Documents When You Delegate (Laban, Schnabel and Neville, Microsoft Research, April 2026)
Scale
19 models, 52 professional domains, 310 work environments, 20 delegated edits each
Result
Content kept after 20 edits: Gemini 3.1 Pro 80.9%, Claude 4.6 Opus 73.1%, GPT 5.4 71.5%. All 19 models averaged about 50% degradation.
Readiness
“Ready” means keeping 98% or more. The best model was ready in 11 of 52 domains. Only Python was ready for most models.

Rules versus AI for text from data

Rule-based systems made fewer meaning errors than neural and AI systems, though rules still need testing.

A study of 15 systems that turn structured data into text counted omissions, additions and repetitions in 30 outputs each.

System typeErrors per 30 outputs
Rule- and template-based (3 systems)27 to 36
GPT-3.5, no fine-tuning39
Llama-2 70B, no fine-tuning57
Fine-tuned neural systems (10)25 to 86

Limits. The gap was modest and the LLMs were older. The lesson for Chart Details: rules are only as good as their coverage, so each rule needs tests.

Huidrom, Belz and Lorandi, INLG 2024 · aclanthology.org/2024.inlg-main.47 · Read in full

What blind readers want

Blind readers asked for facts about the data. Those facts are the easiest content to compute from data.

  • 30 blind and 90 sighted readers ranked chart descriptions. Most blind readers wanted descriptions to “stick to straight facts about the data,” without editorializing.
  • The authors define four levels of content. Levels 1 and 2 (what the chart encodes, plus statistics such as extremes and comparisons) can be computed directly when software has the data. Producing them from an image is “considerably more difficult.”
  • They recommend descriptions that convey overall trends and statistics.

Lundgard and Satyanarayan, IEEE VIS 2021 · vis.mit.edu/pubs/vis-text-model.pdf · Read in full
