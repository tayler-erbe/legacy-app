# Competitive landscape & where Legacy can win

*Research snapshot: November 2026. Many sources in this space are written by
competitors or are SEO comparison pages, so prices and claims are directional.
Links are at the bottom. Verify anything before quoting it publicly.*

---

## TL;DR

The market splits into four camps, and each fails in a predictable way:

| Camp | Examples | What they get right | How they fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prompt → printed book** | Storyworth, Remento, StoryKeeper, Kindred Tales | Simple weekly prompts; a tangible gift at the end | Feels like homework, so many books never get finished. Email-dependent. AI rewrites drift from the storyteller's real words. Ends after a year. |
| **Record → ask later** | HereAfter AI, StoryFile, Eternos | Real voice, real answers; family can "ask" questions | **The companies don't last.** HereAfter reportedly shut down in 2026, StoryFile went bankrupt in 2024, and Eternos pivoted away to become Uare.ai. |
| **Generative "griefbots"** | 2wai, Séance AI, You Only Virtual, Project December | Emotionally powerful demos | Public backlash ("demonic"), weak consent, unsettled research on whether they help or harm grief, and they say things the person never said. |
| **Platform features** | MyHeritage LiveMemory, Apple Legacy Contact, Google Inactive Account Manager | Scale, trust, and labelling (watermarked AI video) | They hand over *data*, not *stories*. Nobody captures the meaning. |

**Legacy's opening:** be the one that is still readable in 50 years, keeps the
person's real voice, is easy enough that stories actually get told, and treats
any AI as a careful librarian of what they said, not a puppet.

## 1. Prompt → printed book

**Storyworth** is the category leader: weekly email prompts for a year, then a
hardcover. Prices: about $59–$109 per year, or $199 for Unlimited (sources
conflict). Trustpilot is strong at 4.5★ on about 63k reviews, but the recurring
complaints are consistent:

- **Completion.** Typing an answer every week "feels like homework," and many
  storytellers stall. One quoted Reddit comment: their parents didn't answer a
  single question for a year.
- **Fragile delivery.** The weekly emails sometimes just stop.
- Rigid layout and editing tools, billing problems at renewal, and no free
  corrections.

**Remento** (Shark Tank) is about $99 per year. You speak or record video,
"Speech-to-Story" turns it into prose, and the book has QR codes that play the
original audio.

- People love talking instead of typing.
- **But** reviewers say the AI rewrite can differ noticeably from what was
  actually said, the transcripts are weak, and support is thin.
- Editing access ends when the subscription does.
- It reportedly pulled its native apps in June 2025 and is now web-only.

**StoryKeeper** is about $99 one-time, with prompts and two hardcovers. It's a
quiet signal that one-time pricing appeals in a gift market.

**Lessons for Legacy**
1. Voice-first beats type-first. We already lead with the record button. Keep it.
2. **Never let AI replace the original words.** Store the raw audio and a
   verbatim transcript as the source of truth. Any "polished" version is an
   optional layer and is labelled as such.
3. Completion is the real product problem. Make it smaller (one moment, not a
   memoir), more social (family asks), and continuous (not a one-year box).
4. Never hold editing or export hostage to a subscription.

## 2. Record → ask later

**HereAfter AI** used a virtual interviewer that recorded real audio answers,
and family could later "ask" questions and hear the recordings. It cost
$3.99–$7.99 per month or $99–$199 one-time, and full MP3 export was only on the
top tier. Coverage from a competitor reports that it **announced a shutdown in
July 2026**, with the domain returning page-not-found and the iOS app last
updated in September 2023. (I couldn't independently confirm the shutdown.)

**StoryFile** made interactive video "conversations" (the AI William Shatner).
It **filed Chapter 11 in May 2024**, and the sale of its assets was contested by
the US Trustee. Public reporting doesn't say what happened to users' recordings.

**Eternos** built a full interactive AI of a person, with "legacy advisors"
interviewing them. It **pivoted in November 2025** into Uare.ai, a personal AI
assistant, after raising a $10.3M seed round. The legacy use case was not the
business.

**Character.AI** isn't a legacy app, but it **filed Chapter 11 in October 2026**.
The Disney licence excludes user data, and whether users' chats are sold is
unresolved. Forty state attorneys general intervened.

**Lessons for Legacy.** This is the single most important finding. **The entire
premise of these products is "forever," and they keep dying within a few
years.** People are trusting a startup with their dead mother's voice. So:

1. **Survivability is the product, not a footnote.** Legacy must work, and the
   exports must be readable, even if Legacy (the company) disappears tomorrow.
   We already store data locally and export openly; the next step is a
   **self-contained offline "book" export** (HTML + media files) that opens in
   any browser with no app.
2. Publish a **shutdown promise**: if we ever wind down, every user gets a
   full export and at least 12 months' notice, and user data is never an asset
   for sale. Put it in the terms of service.
3. Export should be free on every tier, always. HereAfter gating MP3 export
   was a mistake.

## 3. Generative "griefbots"

**2wai** (HoloAvatar) builds an interactive avatar from a few minutes of
video. It got heavy backlash over consent ("demonic") despite claims of
on-device processing and family approval. **Séance AI, Re;memory and You Only
Virtual** sell subscription chatbots of people who have died (July 2026
reporting). Voice-clone legacy apps are multiplying too: Living Forever AI got
an ElevenLabs grant, and there's VoCapsule and Evervoice.

**What the research says**
- The Cambridge Leverhulme Centre's 2024 paper *Griefbots, Deadbots,
  Postmortem Avatars* recommends:
  - consent from **both** the person whose data is used and the people who
    will talk to the bot;
  - clear disclosure that it's AI;
  - adults only;
  - no bots that reach out unprompted or post on social media;
  - a dignified way to **retire** a bot (a "digital funeral");
  - no contracts that stop families from switching it off.
- The 2025–2026 literature is **mostly theoretical and perception-based**. Bots
  are seen as neither inherently helpful nor harmful. They can support
  "continuing bonds," but there are real worries about inauthenticity and
  dependence. **No good controlled studies exist yet.** One 2026 paper's point
  stands out: the vendor controls what the avatar says and who can end it.

**Lessons for Legacy**
1. **Don't build a puppet. Build a librarian.** Stage 3 chat should *retrieve
   and play back* the person's actual recordings and quote their actual words,
   with a citation ("from *The lake, summer 1987*"). When there's no story,
   it should say so ("She never recorded that one") instead of inventing an
   answer. That is ethically defensible, harder for critics to attack, and
   *more* emotionally real, because it's actually them.
2. Generative "in their voice" replies, if ever offered, should be:
   - opt-in by the **living** person, in writing, for that specific use;
   - always labelled as AI;
   - never able to start a conversation on its own.
3. Ship the Cambridge recommendations as product features, not policies: a
   consent screen, an AI label, adults only, a retire button.

## 4. Platform features

- **MyHeritage LiveMemory** (2024) animates whole photos into short videos,
  following its earlier Deep Nostalgia (2021). Notably, it **watermarks every
  video and says plainly it's an AI reconstruction**. That's the right norm to
  copy if we ever animate anything.
- **Apple Legacy Contact / Google Inactive Account Manager** hand over account
  *access* after death (Apple: up to 5 contacts, a death certificate plus an
  access key, about 3 years of access). Neither is on by default, and both
  deliver a raw pile of data with no context about what mattered.

**Lesson:** the gap between "here are 40,000 photos" and "here is why this one
mattered" is exactly what Legacy fills. Make it easy to bring photos *in* from
those libraries and attach the story.

## 5. Market & pricing context

- Size estimates vary roughly threefold depending on definitions: "digital
  legacy" at about $22–26B in 2026, and "digital afterlife" projected at about
  $80B by 2034. StartUs counts about 950 "death tech" companies, about 160
  funded, with about $837M raised in total. Treat all of this as directional.
- **Price anchors:** Storyworth about $59–199 per year, Remento $99 per year,
  StoryKeeper $99 one-time, HereAfter $4–8 per month.
- `ARCHITECTURE.md` assumes **$10–20 per month**. That's well above every
  comparable product. Consider instead:
  - **Free** local app, full export always included;
  - a **one-time "book"** purchase (print or a beautiful digital edition),
    which is the proven, gift-driven revenue;
  - an **optional subscription** only for things that cost us money to run:
    sync/backup, transcription, and the chat.

## 6. How Legacy beats them: concrete product moves

Ordered by leverage. ✅ marks items already shipped.

### A. Make stories actually get told (the completion problem)
- ✅ One-moment capture, voice-first, story prompts.
- **"Ask me" links.** Anyone in the family can send a link like
  `…/?ask=How did you meet Dad?`, which opens Legacy with that question
  pinned. No backend needed. Questions from your own daughter beat generic
  prompts, and it turns a solo chore into a family conversation.
  Storyworth and Remento are one-directional.
- **Gentle rhythm without email.** A "remind me weekly" button that adds a
  recurring calendar event (an `.ics` file). It works offline, needs no
  server, and avoids the "the emails just stopped" failure.
- **Continuous, not a one-year box.** No arbitrary end date. Seasonal "On this
  day" resurfacing (✅) keeps people coming back.

### B. Survive anything
- ✅ Local-first storage, open JSON export, restore, persistent storage, backup
  nudges.
- **Offline "book" export.** A zip containing `index.html`, `images/` and
  `audio/` that reads like a beautiful book in any browser, forever, with no
  app. This is the answer to HereAfter and StoryFile, and it doubles as a
  shareable digital edition.
- A public **shutdown promise** in the terms of service.

### C. Keep their real voice
- Raw audio plus a verbatim transcript as the permanent record. An optional,
  labelled "tidied" version. A side-by-side view so nothing drifts.

### D. A legacy plan, built in
- In Settings, name **who** gets the vault, **when** (on request, after death,
  or on a date), and **what mode**:
  1. archive only;
  2. "ask" mode that plays back recordings;
  3. no AI, ever.
- Print a one-page "how to open my Legacy" sheet that goes with a will.
- A **retire** option the family controls.

### E. AI as librarian (Stage 3)
- Retrieval-only answers grounded in recordings, with citations and playback.
  An explicit "they never told this story" when there's nothing to draw on.
  Adults only. Labelled.
- Use the gaps: "Nobody's asked about your twenties yet. Want to tell one?"
  That turns the chat into a capture engine, not just a reader.

### F. Visual & brand
- Use Higgsfield-style tools for *our* artwork (icon, onboarding, empty
  states, promo video), never for users' photos without explicit opt-in.
- If photo animation ever ships, copy MyHeritage: always watermarked and
  labelled as an AI reconstruction.
